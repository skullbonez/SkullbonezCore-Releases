# SkullbonezCore 2026.09.27

This release brings a new glitch-skull logo and sharper header, a connected Options workspace, clearer graphics and material controls, stronger Jeep collisions, terrain undo, and smoother movement when inspecting a predicted collision.

## Download and run

Extract the entire Windows ZIP to a writable folder. Run **Launch SkullbonezCore.cmd**, or **SKULLBONEZ_CORE.exe** from that folder. Keep **SkullbonezData** and all DLLs beside it. You need 64-bit Windows and a DirectX 12-compatible GPU and driver; no development tools are required. This portable build is unsigned.

The included **SkullbonezCore-First-Time-User-Manual.pdf** has updated controls. Click the **Tutorial book** in the top bar for guided practice. Optional Solver Lab comparison recordings are omitted from the ZIP.

## Find the reorganized controls

1. Click **Options** at the bottom. One connected panel opens with scene tools on the left and diagnostics on the right.
2. Use **Scene** on the left to load a level; **Keys & Help** lists controls. **Video** on the right contains the graphics presets and switches described below. Each side scrolls independently.
3. Drag the central grip along the panel's top edge to change its height. The two halves keep equal widths; narrow tab lists wrap.
4. Click **Options** again or the far-right **X** to close both halves. Their selected tabs are remembered.

The top bar now shows the new logo, a readable **Level:** title and the nearby **Edit mode / Playback** button. At the right are the camera mode and icons for the Tutorial book, four/single view, hiding the interface, and fullscreen/windowed. Hover for help. The header remains visible during an active tutorial, including in Canvas layout. The tutorial's minus button collapses its card; plus or the book expands it again.

## Choose graphics quality and keep your settings

1. Open **Options > Video** on the right.
2. Choose **Max**, **Medium**, or **Min** in the preset dropdown. Watch the live FPS and frame-time readout as you try a choice.
3. Adjust **VSync**, **Shadows**, **Sky effects and bloom**, **Rounded corners**, or the lighting and material switches individually. An adjusted preset becomes **Custom**. Rounded corners changes shading, not the physical collision shape.
4. Use **Reflection** to choose **Planar**, **Off**, or **Ray traced (experimental)**. Presets do not enable ray tracing; choosing a preset returns it to the preset's reflection choice.
5. Click **Save settings** if you want the graphics choices after restarting. Until then the panel says **Unsaved: this run only**. Experimental ray-traced reflection remains a choice for the current run only.

The graphics Shadows switch now controls whether shadows are drawn across scenes. Medium removes some costly lighting features; Min also removes shadows and reflections and reduces effects. Choose the result that works best on your hardware.

For the overall colour and lighting style, open **Render** on the right or **Appearance** on the left. The **Look** dropdown offers **Clean**, **Studio**, **Vivid**, **Soft daylight**, **Crisp**, and **Halo glow**. Pick a style, then use that panel's **Save settings** to keep it. The dropdown now selects all its styles correctly.

**Min-spec physics** has moved to **Physics > Simulation**. It is independent of the graphics preset and is remembered automatically: on uses 60 Hz with four contact iterations; off uses 120 Hz with twelve. Lower physics quality can make complex stacks less stable and changes the simulation, so turn it off when you want the normal-quality result.

## Change a surface's finish

1. Enter **Edit mode**, release right mouse, turn **Modify velocity** off, and choose **Select [Q]**.
2. Select an object and open **Editor > Material**. Use **Selected object**, or choose **Ground** to change the ground surface instead.
3. Choose a **Finish**, such as **Matte**, **Glossy**, **Rubber**, **Varnished wood**, **Ceramic**, **Brushed metal**, **Chrome**, or **Glow**. Changing the finish keeps your selected colour.
4. Adjust **Roughness** and **Reflectivity**; **More adjustments** exposes further controls. **Stylised looks** contains the decorative surface recipes. Changes apply immediately; **Ctrl+Z** undoes an edit.
5. Use **Save scene** in Editor Controls to keep the authored result. Saved colour slots and material copy/paste are also available in the Material tab.

Lighting now gives surfaces clearer ambient shading and contact depth. Ground roughness and reflectivity affect its mirror appearance. Coloured box edges keep their colour at grazing angles, and the bright seam along two-tone box faces has been corrected.

## Edit, play, and resize the camera dock

1. Click **Edit mode / Playback** in the header, or press **backtick** (the US keyboard key below Esc). Clicking an Editor control also enters editing.
2. In Edit mode, the lower dock shows **Camera**. In Playback, the same space shows **Replay**. Drag the small grip between Editor and that dock to change their relative heights; the split is remembered.
3. Click the main **Play** control to leave Edit mode, return to the Scene camera and run the simulation. The timeline becomes available as time is recorded or predicted.

Director paths remain available: while editing, hold **right mouse + W/A/S/D** to move, release right mouse, and **Capture view** at two or more positions in Camera. Choose **Curve**, an easing option and **Faster / Slower**, then **Play path**. **Space** pauses/resumes. Hold **B** to grab the view temporarily; releasing it blends back. **Backtick** returns to editing after the preview. **Save path / Load path** use a separate `.shot.json` file.

Free flight and the four-view starting cameras are better framed for nearby objects. Right mouse looks, W/A/S/D moves while it is held, Shift boosts speed, and the wheel while looking adjusts speed. Release right mouse to use the editor.

## Sculpt terrain with undo

1. Create or load a writable level, enter Edit mode and open **Editor > Terrain**.
2. Enable **Terrain brush**, choose **Brush: Raise ground / Lower ground** and set **Brush radius**.
3. Hold left mouse over the terrain and drag. Each completed drag is one undoable stroke.
4. Press **Ctrl+Z** to undo or **Ctrl+Y / Ctrl+Shift+Z** to redo. Terrain appearance and collision update together.
5. Click **Save scene** to keep the terrain for the next launch. Tutorial practice scenes are protected; use your own level for saved work.

## Collision and replay fixes

- The Jeep now collides with its body and four rounded tyres, including objects contacting the roof and sides. Try **tutorial_drive.scene.json**. Double-click the vehicle to attach, press **T** for Drive, then use **W/A/S/D** and **Space** to drive and brake; **Esc** detaches.
- Very heavy dynamic objects now respond to fixed obstacles correctly. This fixes the heavy ball passing through its launcher block in **tutorial_landscape.scene.json**.
- Clicking a predicted cause now moves the displayed bodies more smoothly toward the inspected collision. The transition ends on the selected simulation state; ordinary playback and scrubbing retain their usual behavior.
- Tutorial highlights follow the current controls, the collapsed card reopens properly, and its initial camera is closer to the practice objects.

## Notes and feedback

**Save scene**, **Replay > Save recording**, and **Camera > Save path** write different things. **F2** makes a separate scene shot; it does not save the active level. Save your scene before loading another level.

Ray-traced reflection is experimental. Frame rate depends on the scene, selected effects and hardware. Solver Lab's optional comparison recordings are not included in this download.

Please include this version, the scene, your steps and what happened in the [public issue tracker](https://github.com/skullbonez/SkullbonezCore-Releases/issues).
