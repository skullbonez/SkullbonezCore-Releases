# SkullbonezCore 2026.09.29 - Major release

This major release protects unsaved scenes, adds selectable physics solvers and live Solver Lab comparisons, and improves ragdolls, camera controls, grass and rendering.

## Download and run

Extract the entire Windows x64 ZIP into a writable folder. Run **Launch SkullbonezCore.cmd** or **SKULLBONEZ_CORE.exe** there. Keep **SkullbonezData**, **ThirdPtySource** and the DLLs beside the executable. You need 64-bit Windows and a DirectX 12-compatible GPU and driver; no development tools are required. This build is unsigned.

Read the included **SkullbonezCore-First-Time-User-Manual.pdf**, or open **Tutorial** in the top bar for guided practice. Optional Solver Lab comparison recordings are omitted from the ZIP; live comparisons work without them.

## Keep your scene when leaving

1. Edit a scene, then choose **New scene**, load another scene through **Options > Scene**, or close the window.
2. Outside Tutorial, if work is unsaved, a skull dialog appears above the whole interface. **Save and continue** saves before replacing the scene; **Save and close** saves before exiting. **Discard** leaves without saving. **Cancel**, or **Esc**, keeps your scene open.
3. The background controls stay blocked while you decide. If saving fails, the scene stays open so you can retry or cancel. An untouched demo does not ask you to save just because you choose New scene.
4. You can still save at any time with the top-bar **SAVE**, **Ctrl+S**, or **Editor > Controls > Save scene**. Check the confirmation notice.

While Tutorial is active, save prompts are suppressed so lesson transitions and closing the app are not interrupted.

New user levels and saved demo/tutorial copies now go in **Documents/SkullbonezCore/Levels**. The scene browser lists these alongside bundled scenes. Existing files continue to save to their current locations; older levels are not moved automatically. Keep a copy of older install folders if they contain your scenes.

**Esc no longer exits the application**, and closing no longer displays a thank-you popup. Left/right arrows no longer change scenes. Use the scene browser instead; arrows still step comparisons inside Solver Lab. **Play** leaves Edit mode automatically.

## Ragdolls in the demo and editor

The generated Mixed demo now includes ragdolls starting higher above the scene. Newly generated ragdolls have buoyant masses so they float in water; existing saved masses are preserved.

1. Enter **Edit mode** and choose **Select [Q]**. Release camera mouse-look before selecting.
2. Click a ragdoll body part and press **Delete**. The whole ragdoll is removed together.
3. **Ctrl+Z** restores it, including its joints; **Ctrl+Y** or **Ctrl+Shift+Z** removes it again. Save to keep the result after reopening.
4. Open **Physics > Visualisation > Ragdoll colliders** to inspect **Selected Ragdolls** or **All Ragdolls**.

In **Options > Profiler**, **Frame** starts expanded. Ragdoll neck-limit work has its own marker under **Frame > Physics > Ragdolls > Neck limits**.

## Choose a physics solver

There are eleven solver choices, including Skullbonez, PGS/TGS variants, XPBD, Soft Step and Jacobi. They can produce different stack, contact and joint behavior; Skullbonez remains the default.

1. Load a scene, save any edits, and return to the live scene rather than a loaded recording or comparison.
2. Open **Physics > Simulation**. Pick a solver in the chooser at the top and adjust its settings if desired. Hover controls for their meaning and range.
3. Click **Apply & restart with <solver>** (or **Apply & restart** in a narrow panel). The pending choice becomes active and the scene restarts. **Revert** abandons pending settings.
4. **Restart with <solver>** repeats the current setup from its start. The choice lasts for this session. Click **Save Physics defaults** farther down Simulation to keep the applied solver and settings for future launches; changing the chooser alone does not apply them.

Object friction now resists sliding at contacts instead of damping motion through the air. Existing scenes may therefore move differently from older releases. The included **at_rest.scene.json** is a simple starting point; the scene browser also includes wall and solver examples.

## Compare solvers live

1. Load the scene you want to compare, then open **Options > Scene > Solver Lab**. It starts from that scene's authored beginning.
2. In **Controls > Live solvers**, choose each lane's solver with its arrows. Use two to four lanes. Check the **From** control to choose the scene start or current moment before starting.
3. Press **Start live lanes**. Use **L** and **R** to select the lanes shown in the two views. The copies use the scene's world forces and run independently of your original scene.
4. Pause and step to compare a single physics tick. **Follow A** follows the selected object during playback and timeline scrubbing. Dragging the timeline or operating a panel no longer moves the camera behind it.
5. Save the live session to reopen the comparison later, or stop it to discard the lanes. Leave Solver Lab to return to your scene. Recorded comparison files remain a separate save from editable levels.

## Camera and Minecraft building

In the normal viewport, hold **right mouse** to look, use **W/A/S/D** to fly and **Q/E** to move down/up. Hold **Shift** for faster travel; the wheel while looking adjusts travel speed. Nearby objects no longer make free-flight speed change unexpectedly. Release right mouse before using editor shortcuts.

For Minecraft building:

1. Open **Editor > Controls**, enable **Minecraft mode**, and click the viewport to capture the pointer.
2. **W/S** travels level with the ground; **A/D** moves sideways. **Left-click** places and **right-click** removes. Short clicks now register reliably.
3. The block-sized placement grid extends farther around you. **Wheel** zooms, **Shift-wheel** changes placement height, and **Ctrl-wheel** rotates the preview.
4. **Esc** releases the pointer. **1 / 2 / 3** select Move / Rotate / Scale; **4** turns 90 degrees, **5** snaps to ground, and **6** resets the scene. Choose an object in **Objects** to resume placement, then save your work.

## Visual and usability changes

- Grass has improved lighting, flattening and recovery, including recorded deformation when rewinding. Turn **Grass** on in **Options > Video** to see it; it starts off. Use **Save settings** to keep graphics choices across launches.
- Glossy primitives, rounded-box shadows, depth handling and overlapping transparent objects have rendering fixes. Water reflections now use **Planar** or **Off**; the experimental ray-traced reflection path has been removed.
- Hover help now covers the interface consistently. Profiler history is bounded, and scene restart and recording saves have reliability fixes.

## Notes and feedback

Save the editable scene separately from **Replay > Save recording**, **Camera > Save path**, or a Solver Lab session. **F2** still makes a separate scene shot. Frame rate and solver behavior depend on the scene, settings and hardware; alternative solvers remain tools for comparison, not promises of identical results. Old recordings may not be compatible with changed simulation or rendering formats.

Report problems in the [public issue tracker](https://github.com/skullbonez/SkullbonezCore-Releases/issues), including this version, your scene, the steps and what happened.

Solver Lab's optional comparison recordings are not included in this download.
