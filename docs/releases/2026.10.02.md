# SkullbonezCore 2026.10.02 - Water, space scenes and editor improvements

This release adds moving water with wakes and replay scrubbing, new space showcases, searchable controls, editable object Properties, reverse playback, and improvements to rendering and physics performance.

## Download and run

Extract the entire Windows x64 ZIP into a writable folder. Run **Launch SkullbonezCore.cmd** or **SKULLBONEZ_CORE.exe**. Keep **SkullbonezData**, **ThirdPtySource** and the DLLs beside it. Requires 64-bit Windows and a compatible DirectX 12 GPU and driver. This portable build is unsigned.

Open **Tutorial** in the header for guided practice. The included **SkullbonezCore-First-Time-User-Manual.pdf** covers the fundamentals; the changed controls and panel locations below supersede its older labels. Keep a backup of your scenes and old install folder before saving them in this version.

## Find controls and use the reorganized panels

1. Click **Search controls...** in the header or press **Ctrl+K**. Type a control, object or material name.
2. Choose a result to open its panel, scroll to it and briefly highlight it. Object results enter editing and arm that object for placement. Press **Esc** to close search.
3. Open **Options** for the bottom drawer. Its left side contains **Scene**, **World**, **Look**, **Sky & Cinematic**, and **Keys & Help**. The right side contains **Profiler**, **CPU**, **Memory**, **Allocations**, **Targets**, and **Graphics**.
4. **Build** is now in the Editor dock beside **Objects**. The object palette is grouped, and **Place as static (fixed in place)** controls whether newly placed objects move.

The header mode button now reads **PLAYING** or **EDITING**. **SAVE/SAVED** shows the save state. Use **Graphics > Interface size** to adjust the interface; native monitor scaling also makes text and the viewport crisper. Use **Save settings** to retain graphics choices. Press **0** to show or hide the interface.

## Edit an object's Properties

1. Switch to **EDITING**, release camera mouse-look, and use **Select [Q]** to select an object.
2. Expand **Properties**, the lower pane of the right dock. Adjust position, rotation, size, mass/density, restitution, static/sleeping state or velocity where available. Friction is a readout because the world setting controls it.
3. Changes support **Ctrl+Z** to undo and **Ctrl+Y** to redo. With several objects selected, the Properties pane identifies the last selected object being edited.
4. Bomb, tornado, rocket and buggy settings are here too. Use **SAVE** or **Ctrl+S** to keep authored changes after reopening the level. During playback these property fields are disabled.

Reset and entering Edit now restore authored bodies removed by the kill plane, world boundary or a black hole. Unsaved edits survive Reset; save the scene to retain them between launches.

## Water that moves with the replay

1. Load a water scene such as **water_ball_test.scene.json**, then press **Play**. Surface ripples, impact rings and wakes accompany moving bodies. Reflections are weaker from above and stronger at grazing angles.
2. Open **Options > Look > Water**. The sliders control reflection, distortion, surface detail, movement, clarity, colour absorption and rings. Hover a slider for its meaning and range.
3. **Reset water** restores the built-in slider defaults. **Save settings** retains the Look and Water slider choices for future launches; without saving, they last for this run. Scene sky, lighting and water colour use **Sky & Cinematic**'s save controls.
4. Pause or scrub recorded time: the water surface follows the presented time, including backwards. The recent ring history is bounded, so very old splashes may no longer appear.

The Graphics water-reflection setting must be enabled for reflected objects. Hidden or disabled water cannot display the surface effects.

## Reverse playback, Director cameras and forecasts

1. Press **Play** to record some simulation, then **Pause**. Use **Reverse** to move backwards through retained history, **Play** to move forward, and **Step** for a single simulation tick. These controls sit together on the replay transport.
2. In the **Camera** controls, a path's timing can follow **Scene time** or run **Independent**. With Scene time, a playing path follows scrubbing and reverse playback. Independent paths use their own **Play path** control. **Save path** keeps the path and timing choice in its separate shot file.
3. Forecast controls are in **Replay**. Choose the intended scope before starting a forecast. Space forecast lines are steadier, with additional display choices for their paths and markers.

Ordinary free-camera positioning stays put when branching recorded simulation. Followed objects now have an outline aligned to their displayed pose.

For normal navigation, hold **right mouse** and use **W/A/S/D** to fly, **Q/E** down/up, **Shift** to boost, and the wheel while looking to change speed. Release right mouse before editor shortcuts. **Middle drag** pans; **Alt-left drag** orbits and **Alt-right drag** dollies. In Minecraft mode, **Esc** releases captured mouse-look before using panels.

## Space and object showcases

Open **Options > Scene** to choose bundled scenes:

- **starlink.scene.json**: press **Play** to begin the authored Director flyby and its forecast. Pause and scrub to inspect the scene; choose another camera mode to leave the authored view.
- **solar_system.scene.json** and **solar_system_mars_slingshot.scene.json**: updated space presentation, Milky Way sky, Sun glare and a visible rocket.
- **solar_system_sun_death.scene.json**: a deliberately compressed, illustrative sequence from red giant through collapse and infall. Press Play to run it; its sequence follows simulation time and can be revisited through recorded playback. It is a visual demonstration, not a realistic timescale model.
- **skull_logo_candidates.scene.json**: five destructible skull designs. Hull contact and debris work have performance improvements; results still depend on hardware and scene load.
- **cine_pyramid_*.scene.json**: new material and lighting showcases.

The **Rocket** object is available in the palette's Effects group. Place one in Edit, select it, and use **Properties** for countdown, burn time, thrust and launch tilt. Save the scene, then Play to launch it. Its smoke and flame follow recorded playback. Buggy tuning also lives in Properties; the buggy now has all-wheel drive and speed-sensitive steering.

## Graphics and reliability

All scenes use the cinematic rendering path. **Graphics > Post effects** includes auto exposure, contact shadows, depth of field, anti-aliasing and lens flare. SMAA is the default; TAA and depth of field are optional. **Cascaded shadows** is optional and starts off. Quality presets can suppress costly effects; use Save settings to keep your choices.

Grass has less shimmer and lower rendering cost. Shadow stability, terrain relief, water edges and ambient occlusion at screen edges are improved. Stack contact handling and replay recording have performance and reliability fixes. Prediction preparation is spread across frames to reduce toggle stalls. Bodies and predicted paths leaving the supported world now end instead of triggering the previous boundary failure.

## Solver Lab and saved work

Use the header **SOLVER LAB** button for live comparisons. Choose lane solvers and start the live lanes; pause or step to inspect their differences. Saved comparison files can still be opened, but the old bundled recorded comparison library has been removed. Solver choices need not produce identical results.

Save editable scenes separately from recordings, camera paths and Solver Lab sessions. New personal levels and demo/tutorial copies go in **Documents/SkullbonezCore/Levels**. Outside Tutorial, the save/discard/cancel prompt protects unsaved changes when leaving. During Tutorial those prompts are suppressed. Older recordings may not be compatible with changed formats; preserve the earlier application if you need to revisit them.

Solver Lab's optional comparison recordings are not included in this download.

Please report problems in the [public issue tracker](https://github.com/skullbonez/SkullbonezCore-Releases/issues), including this version, scene name and the steps you took.
