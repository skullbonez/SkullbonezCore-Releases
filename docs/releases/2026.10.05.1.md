# SkullbonezCore 2026.10.05.1 - Water swell, shoreline foam and Director tools

This release makes water part of the action: rolling swell now moves the visible surface and the Physics surface together, floating bodies ride the waves, and editable shoreline foam follows the moving waterline. It also adds scene-timed Director viewpoints, a side-by-side second-solver preview, improved ragdoll settling, turf grass by default, a low-frame-rate setup offer, and a substantial collection of replay, editor, rendering and performance fixes.

## Download and run

Extract the entire Windows x64 ZIP into a writable folder. Run **Launch SkullbonezCore.cmd** or **SKULLBONEZ_CORE.exe**. Keep **SkullbonezData**, **ThirdPtySource** and the DLLs beside the executable. The application requires 64-bit Windows and a compatible DirectX 12 GPU and driver. This build is unsigned, so Windows or antivirus software may show an unfamiliar-app warning.

Open **Tutorial** in the header for guided practice. The included **SkullbonezCore-First-Time-User-Manual.pdf** covers the fundamentals. These notes focus on the newest controls. The [2026.10.03 notes](https://github.com/skullbonez/SkullbonezCore-Releases/releases/tag/v2026.10.03) describe the retained independent-timing Director controls, Box3D solver, editor fixes and replay improvements.

## Shape the water and shoreline

Open a scene with water, such as **water_ball_test.scene.json**, then open **Options > Appearance > Water**.

1. Adjust **Swell height** to raise or lower the waves. A value of 0 makes the surface flat. Floating bodies use the same moving Physics surface that is drawn, rather than a separate flat plane.
2. Use **Swell length** for the crest-to-crest scale, **Swell speed** for motion, and **Swell direction** for the travel heading. A speed of 0 freezes the wave shape in place.
3. Adjust **Foam amount**, **Foam width**, **Foam brightness**, **Foam scale** and **Foam drift**. Foam follows water depth, so it forms a wide band on a gentle beach and a narrow band against a steep bank. Impact rings and wakes fade as they reach it instead of continuing onto land.
4. Choose **Revert default** at the top of Water to restore the built-in Water values for this run. This does not write settings immediately.
5. Choose **Save settings** to retain the current look after restarting. Without saving, the adjustments last only for the current run.

Open **Physics > Visualisation** and enable **Water surface** to draw the collision surface as a wire grid around the camera. Blue marks troughs and white marks crests. This is useful when checking why a floating object rises, falls or stays awake.

Water edits are operator settings and no longer discard the current replay. Replay restoration and prediction retain the swell clock, so recorded bodies return to the wave phase they originally used.

## Direct a scene by time and by subject

The Camera dock now supports scene-timed viewpoints. The updated **solar_system_sun_death.scene.json** is the most complete example: its Director path follows the planetary collapse, rides selected bodies and reaches the black-hole finale with a controlled gravity-grid and forecast presentation.

1. Enter **EDITING**, open **Camera**, and move the replay or live scene to the moment where a shot should begin.
2. Choose **Insert viewpoint** or press **Insert**. The current camera and scene time become a new viewpoint. On an empty path this starts a scene-timed path.
3. Select an object in the editor, then choose **Attach to selected** or press **U** to make the viewpoint ride that object. Use the same control with no selection, or on an already attached viewpoint, to return it to world space. The camera does not jump when attachment changes.
4. Choose **Anchor here** or press **T** to move the selected viewpoint to the current scene time. Viewpoints remain in time order and cannot share the same time.
5. Press **End** or choose **Next**. The scene plays forward to the next viewpoint while the camera glides there, then holds. Press **Home** or choose **Previous** to seek back through recorded time.
6. While held at a viewpoint, hold **B** to grab the camera, adjust the framing, then choose **Capture viewpoint** or press **J** to store the new framing. An attached view keeps that framing relative to its body.
7. Press **Delete** or choose **Remove viewpoint** to remove the selected point. Use **Save path** or **L** to keep the path in the scene's separate `.shot.json` sidecar. Saving the scene alone does not save the camera path.

The existing independent-timing path remains available. It supports per-viewpoint scene speed from 0x to 4x, speed easing, curved or linear camera motion, **Space** to pause and **Restart path** to return to the beginning. Camera paths hold up to 32 viewpoints.

## Compare a second Physics solver without changing the scene

Open **Physics** while viewing the live simulation. The new **Second solver** chooser predicts the same future again and draws its bodies as amber, transparent ghosts.

1. Leave loaded recordings, historical replay and velocity-edit mode so the live simulation owns the Physics controls.
2. Use the arrows beside **Second solver** to choose any solver except the one currently running. The default is Off.
3. Wait for the amber ghosts to appear. They show the selected solver's prediction at the replay tick on screen; the legend reports its milliseconds per Physics tick.
4. Scrub or play to compare the live solver and the ghosted result. Turning the preview on hides ordinary prediction paths so the two presentations do not compete.
5. Return **Second solver** to Off when finished. This comparison never changes the scene's running solver or saved Physics defaults.

The second solver starts without carried-over contact history, so its first few ticks may differ slightly even when it matches the running solver. Use **SOLVER LAB** for longer recorded comparisons and saved comparison sessions.

## Box3D scheduling and sleep comparison

**Box3D** remains an optional solver in the Physics solver chooser. Its graph-coloured contacts now use the adaptive worker scheduler; **Show colours** displays contact groups and the readout includes the number of threads that solved the last tick. Choose a solver and use **Apply & restart** to run it. **Revert** abandons an unapplied change, while **Save Physics defaults** keeps an applied solver for later launches.

**Physics > Simulation > Box3D sleep** exposes Box3D's sleep rule as an optional comparison for any solver. Turning it on wakes the current bodies and begins a new recording. It is session-only and ignores **Pile stabilization** while active. Turning it off restores the normal Skullbonez support/depth/drift sleep rule, which remains the default.

Ragdolls now settle much more reliably on slopes and in calm water. Jointed contacts retain useful near-surface points, the neck stop conserves momentum, and a waterline anchor lets a floating ragdoll sleep. A ragdoll can still stay awake if it is continually struck or held by other bodies that never settle.

## Grass, shadows and presentation

Grass is now on by default in every scene whose ground material is turf. It uses the turf's own colour and shadowing at distance so the field reads as one even lawn, while nearby blades still bend under moving bodies and leave recoverable tracks. Turn grass off under **Options > Video**, or tune quality, density, distance, height, bend and recovery under **Options > Appearance > Terrain / Materials**. **Revert default** restores the built-in grass values; **Save settings** keeps them after restarting.

Stylised chrome and related finishes no longer receive sun shadows on their unlit back faces. Cinematic shadow depth windows are steadier as the camera moves, reducing abrupt changes around the cascade boundary. Saturn has clearer bands, atmospheric haze, ring gaps and ringshine in the updated space showcase.

## Low-frame-rate offer

On a normal interactive launch, the application can now offer **Turn on min-spec** after sustained low frame rate. Accepting switches on Min-spec graphics and saves the choice; costly clouds, god rays, bloom, edge smoothing, contact shadows, depth of field, lens flare and cascaded shadows turn off. **No thanks** keeps the current graphics and dismisses the one-time offer. You can change the same setting later in **Options > Video**.

This offer changes graphics only. **Physics > Simulation > Min-spec physics** remains a separate setting for the Physics tick rate and contact iterations.

## Replay, editor and reliability fixes

- A replay that stopped because it reached its memory policy can resume after the timeline is reset and the memory policy is changed. The timeline no longer remains as a bare, unusable track in that case.
- Replay profiler markers are committed at safe frame boundaries. Restore probes see the correct swell phase, and current replay artifacts are read with the correct snapshot version.
- Restarting a scene releases a Director hold cleanly. Automation-driven scene loads no longer flash an interactive load-failure popup during validation or performance passes.
- Fatal engine invariants now write `SkullbonezCore_fatal.log` beside the executable and show a clear topmost dialog during an ordinary interactive launch. Tests, tools and automation suppress that dialog.
- **Revert default** buttons are available for Water, Terrain / Materials, Prediction paths and Sky. Sky revert also releases authored overrides so generated behavior such as the demo sun arc can run again. Use the relevant save control to persist the restored defaults.
- The editor's pooled placement recipes, save refusal path, native text input and evidence pop-outs have correctness and layout fixes. Scene-save failures finish once with their actual reason instead of leaving the request pending.
- Replay memory accounting now includes the complete solver state and island contact history. Recordings use the correct encoding for each checkpoint version.

## Performance and resource notes

Worker selection is now based on measured handoff, inline and synchronization costs. Small jobs remain local; sufficiently large Physics work can spread across the available pool. Box3D's block solver is substantially faster on large piles than its serial route on the measured development machine, though the best worker count remains hardware- and scene-dependent.

The UI rendering path is faster and uses less memory in the release comparison. The moving water surface costs additional CPU and GPU time, concentrated around bodies that can actually reach the water; bodies far from the surface skip swell sampling. Grass history uses a fixed snapshot pool so replay can rebuild tracks exactly. The replay recorder permits larger, bounded captures for full-length video work.

## Known limitations and saved files

- Shoreline foam uses perspective view rays. A steeply angled orthographic pane can place the foam band incorrectly, and the tallest grass tips may poke through very shallow foam.
- Water impact rings do not yet replay as independent recorded effects.
- The second-solver preview is a forward prediction, not a second live world. Its first ticks begin without the running solver's accumulated contact history.
- Box3D sleep and the second-solver preview are opt-in and Off by default.
- Large recordings can use substantial memory. Save important recordings and Solver Lab sessions before changing scene or Physics policies.
- Back up personal scenes before saving them with a new version. Editable scenes, recordings, camera paths and Solver Lab sessions are separate files. Personal levels and demo/tutorial copies go in **Documents/SkullbonezCore/Levels**. Older recordings may not be compatible with changed formats; retain the earlier application when you need to open them.

Solver Lab's optional comparison recordings are not included in this download.
