# SkullbonezCore 2026.10.05.2 - Biplane airport, Rube Goldberg chase and responsive grass

This follow-up release adds a spline-flying biplane and airport, a complete Rube Goldberg chase scene, GPU-driven grass that keeps its pressed tracks through replay, and grass coverage on banks up to about 70 degrees. It also adds an Advanced water performance switch and Shore wave fade control, improves the Sun-death finale, and removes slow-motion jitter from body-following cinematic cameras.

## Download and run

Extract the entire Windows x64 ZIP into a writable folder. Run **Launch SkullbonezCore.cmd** or **SKULLBONEZ_CORE.exe**. Keep **SkullbonezData**, **ThirdPtySource** and the DLLs beside the executable. The application requires 64-bit Windows and a compatible DirectX 12 GPU and driver. This build is unsigned, so Windows or antivirus software may show an unfamiliar-app warning.

Open **Tutorial** in the header for guided practice. The included **SkullbonezCore-First-Time-User-Manual.pdf** covers the fundamentals. These notes focus on the additions since 2026.10.05.1.

## Fly the new biplane circuit

Open **biplane_airport.scene.json** for a ready-made airport with a runway, lights, taxiway, apron, hangar, control tower, terminal, windsock, trees and a parked terrain buggy. Its biplane repeatedly takes off, follows a roughly one-minute circuit and lands.

To add a biplane to another scene:

1. Enter **EDITING**, open **Objects**, select **Vehicles**, then choose **Biplane**.
2. Move the placement preview to the circuit's starting point. Rotate the preview before placing it when the runway points in another direction.
3. Click the viewport to place it. The complete flight circuit is translated and rotated with the placed aircraft, so it starts where you clicked and follows the selected heading.
4. Leave Edit or press Play. The biplane flies its authored spline automatically, faces along the route, banks into turns, spins its propeller and loops after landing.
5. Save the scene to retain the placed aircraft and circuit. Copy, paste, undo, redo and scene reload preserve the biplane as one asset instance.

The aircraft follows an authored route rather than aerodynamic flight controls. Its collision body is still physical during each simulation step, so it can knock over objects in its path. A scene supports up to two biplanes. A propeller does not advance while replay is paused.

## Explore the Rube Goldberg chase

Open **rube_goldberg_chase.scene.json** to watch a long event-driven chain reaction. A fast projectile starts the sequence, dominoes and rolling bodies pass the action across the terrain, and the final victim reaches the pond. The scene includes a tuned chase presentation and a pond whose grass reaches the waterline.

Use the normal Replay transport to pause, scrub and replay the chain. Because the shot follows scene events rather than fixed wall-clock moments, its camera changes remain aligned with the action. Body-following shots now interpolate the same presented position used to draw the followed body, removing the one-tick jitter that was most visible during slow motion.

The scene's authored cinematic video data is included for creators using the repository video tools, but ordinary users can simply load and play the scene.

## Grass deformation now runs on the GPU

Moving and rolling bodies press grass into the ground and leave tracks that recover over time. The contact history now lives in GPU event fields instead of being rebuilt across the terrain on the CPU. Replay scrubbing, retained recordings and restored branches present the grass state for the time on screen without the previous CPU reconstruction spikes.

Grass now grows on terrain slopes up to about 70 degrees. Blades on banks grow mostly upward and widen enough to retain coverage, while near-vertical faces remain clear. The Rube Goldberg pond and ramps demonstrate the new slope rule.

Use **Options > Appearance > Terrain / Materials** to adjust **Grass quality**, **Grass density**, **Grass distance**, **Grass height**, **Grass bend** and **Grass recovery**. The **Grass** switch is under **Options > Video > Graphics**. Save settings to keep changes after restarting.

The GPU contact field uses additional video memory: about 272 MiB for the live field, with another field allocated when a loaded recording needs independent grass history. Turning Grass off avoids drawing the blades, but systems with limited VRAM should also avoid unusually large loaded recordings.

## Advanced water and calmer shorelines

**Options > Video > Graphics > Advanced water** is on by default. It enables swell, fine ripples, impact rings, wakes, shoreline foam and distortion. Turning it off selects a cheaper uniform water path that skips those effects. The Min graphics preset and Min-spec graphics turn Advanced water off.

To calm waves at the waterline:

1. Open **Options > Appearance > Water**.
2. Adjust **Shore wave fade**. The default 1.5 m fades fine ripples, swell shading, impact rings and wakes as the water becomes shallower than that depth.
3. Set it to 0 to let those effects continue to the edge. The control needs Advanced water because the cheaper path does not sample shoreline depth.
4. Choose **Save settings** to keep the value after restarting, or **Revert default** to restore the built-in Water values for the current run.

Shore wave fade is separate from **Foam width**: foam width controls how far foam reaches into the water, while Shore wave fade controls how the moving surface settles near land. Foam now defaults to Off; raise **Foam amount** when a scene needs it. Water rendering, reflection, refraction and ring capture are skipped entirely when the highest possible crest is below the terrain.

## Sun-death finale and presentation fixes

The updated **solar_system_sun_death.scene.json** now holds the gravity well after the red giant finishes growing, cuts a clean circular hole at the finale and points the ending camera down into it. The extra funnel and excessive final plunge have been removed, while the Director paths continue to ride the well toward the ending.

Legacy water tint, alpha, reflection and glint rows are hidden from **Sky & Cinematic**. Scenes can still author those values, while current operator adjustments belong under **Appearance > Water**.

## Reliability and project fixes

- Asset instances now save their placed transform, allowing a biplane and its route to reopen where the user placed them.
- Vehicle, timed-field and spline-following registrations share one behaviour owner, so editor placement, paste, undo, redo, deletion and retired-body restoration preserve the appropriate behaviour together.
- Biplane source coverage was added to the project-filter rules, and the scene-parser test project now links the spline-path implementation used by biplane assets.
- Grass starts enabled for older saved preferences that predate the default-on change.
- The even-lawn grass presentation and water shore fade carry updated visual provenance baselines.

## Known limitations and saved files

- Biplanes follow their authored spline; they do not have player flight controls or aerodynamic simulation.
- A scene supports at most two biplanes because each detailed aircraft consumes a large share of the attached-part rendering budget.
- The biplane propeller stands still while replay is paused.
- Grass history uses substantial GPU memory, especially when a loaded recording creates its independent field.
- Shore wave fade affects the refracting Advanced water path only.
- Large recordings can use substantial memory. Save important recordings and Solver Lab sessions before changing scene or Physics policies.
- Back up personal scenes before saving them with a new version. Editable scenes, recordings, camera paths and Solver Lab sessions are separate files. Personal levels and demo/tutorial copies go in **Documents/SkullbonezCore/Levels**. Older recordings may not be compatible with changed formats; retain the earlier application when you need to open them.

Solver Lab's optional comparison recordings are not included in this download.
