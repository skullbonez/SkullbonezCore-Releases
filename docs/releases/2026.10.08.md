# SkullbonezCore 2026.10.08 - Detailed terrain, far grass detail and a faster frame

This release makes the landscape much finer, keeps balls resting on it properly, makes distant grass trails readable, and cuts GPU and CPU frame cost (bloom off by default, half-resolution reflections, an indexed terrain mesh and restored fast shadow sampling). It also splits the profiler into CPU and GPU tabs, lays the Look tab out in two columns, adds a next-generation demo valley, and folds in a large batch of replay, prediction, physics, UI and editor fixes.

## Download and run

Extract the entire Windows x64 ZIP into a writable folder. Run **Launch SkullbonezCore.cmd** or **SKULLBONEZ_CORE.exe**. Keep **SkullbonezData**, **ThirdPtySource** and the DLLs beside the executable. The application requires 64-bit Windows and a compatible DirectX 12 GPU and driver. This build is unsigned, so Windows or antivirus software may show an unfamiliar-app warning.

Open **Tutorial** in the header for guided practice. The included **SkullbonezCore-First-Time-User-Manual.pdf** covers the fundamentals. These notes focus on changes since 2026.10.06.

## Detailed default terrain

The default landscape is now built on a 10 m grid (128 posts a side) instead of 40 m. Rendering, shadows and collision all use the same grid, so hills, ridgelines and terrain shadows are much finer and objects touch exactly the ground you see.

Balls now rest properly on the finer ground. A ball reads the slope across its whole footprint rather than the single facet under its centre, and a ball held still by the ground is allowed to sleep. Open **at_rest.scene.json** and press play: the pile settles within a few seconds on every solver (Skullbonez, PGS and Box3D).

## Faster frames

- **Indexed terrain mesh:** the terrain is drawn with one vertex per grid post and an index list, about six times less terrain vertex work in every pass (colour, each shadow map and reflections). This helps older GPUs most.
- **Bloom is off by default.** Turn it back on in **Sky & Cinematic** if you want the glow.
- **Reflections** now render at half resolution and only shade the rows where they can actually appear, below the horizon.
- **Shadow sampling** is fast again: a shader regression that slowed every shadowed surface is fixed (grass drops from about 0.36 ms to 0.24 ms of GPU time).

## Grass far detail

The **Look** tab has a new **Grass far detail** slider in the terrain and grass section (1.00 to 3.00, default 1.00).

1. Open the **Look** tab and find **Grass far detail** under the grass sliders.
2. Raise it above 1 to keep more grass blades in the distance. Flattened trails left by rolling or sliding objects then stay visible from much farther away.
3. Higher values cost more GPU time; leave it at 1 on slower machines. A crowded view lowers it automatically before running out of capacity.
4. Press **Save settings** to keep your choice after restarting.

## CPU Profile and GPU Profile tabs

The profiler is now two tabs in the diagnostics area:

- **CPU Profile** shows the CPU timeline markers and per-core activity.
- **GPU Profile** shows GPU costs in the same timeline style (per effect: shadow maps, terrain, grass, bloom steps, reflections and more), together with draw-call and GPU statistics.

Use them side by side to see whether a slow frame is CPU or GPU bound.

## Look tab in two columns

The **Look** tab's sliders now sit in two columns on wide windows (prediction paths on the left, terrain and grass on the right), so the panel is shorter and easier to scan. Narrow windows keep a single column.

## Predict from replay history

Predicting while you are looking at replay history now predicts from the frame you are viewing.

1. Load a scene and press play.
2. Press **Play in reverse** (or scrub back) to show an earlier moment.
3. Press **Predict** and click an object: the prediction lines start from the frame on screen.
4. If that frame is too old to branch from, the view returns to the present and shows **PREDICTING FROM LIVE: THAT FRAME IS TOO OLD TO BRANCH**.

## Next-gen demo valley

**claude_next_gen_demo.scene.json** is a new authored demo valley with its own demo loop. Open it from the scene browser and press play.

## Sun death

The Sun death sequence gains its black hole, Director time warp and a 4x camera path. This scene is still being authored and may change between releases.

## Fixes

- Balls no longer creep or jitter on the detailed terrain.
- Many replay, prediction, physics, UI and editor fixes from the follow-up review batches, including solver corrections, ragdoll neck stops and side-lying ragdoll drops.
- Picking, outlines and prediction targets follow the curved horizon at every strength, so clicking an object always selects what is drawn under the cursor.

## Saved files and compatibility

- **engine.cfg** moves to format 16 (the optional **grass_far_detail** row). Older files load unchanged.
- Scenes move to format 13. Older scenes load; saving keeps a copy of the original beside it.
- Back up personal scenes before saving them with a new version. Personal levels and demo/tutorial copies go in **Documents/SkullbonezCore/Levels**. Older recordings may not open after format changes; keep the earlier application if you need them.

## Known limitations

- Grass far detail above 1 increases GPU time; the default keeps the previous cost.
- The Sun death scene is a work in progress.
- Large recordings can use substantial memory. Save important recordings and Solver Lab sessions before changing scenes or Physics policies.

Solver Lab's optional comparison recordings are not included in this download.
