# SkullbonezCore 2026.10.06 - 73 showcase scenes, GPU effects and curved worlds

This release is a major visual and physics-showcase expansion. It adds 73 ready-to-play scenes spanning tornados, sieges, bridges, vehicle stunts, chain reactions, orbital systems and slow-motion destruction; moves water ripples, forecast paths and every particle effect onto the GPU; adds a curved-world presentation; and makes large scenes substantially easier to run on lower-spec graphics settings.

## Download and run

Extract the entire Windows x64 ZIP into a writable folder. Run **Launch SkullbonezCore.cmd** or **SKULLBONEZ_CORE.exe**. Keep **SkullbonezData**, **ThirdPtySource** and the DLLs beside the executable. The application requires 64-bit Windows and a compatible DirectX 12 GPU and driver. This build is unsigned, so Windows or antivirus software may show an unfamiliar-app warning.

Open **Tutorial** in the header for guided practice. The included **SkullbonezCore-First-Time-User-Manual.pdf** covers the fundamentals. These notes focus on additions since 2026.10.05.2.

## 73 new showcase scenes

The scene library now includes a large collection of authored physics demonstrations and cinematic set pieces. Open them through the normal scene browser. Highlights include:

- **nuketown_tornado.scene.json** and **claude_tornado.scene.json** for large debris-filled tornadoes.
- **claude_catapult_siege.scene.json** for four self-reloading catapults bombarding a crate fort.
- **claude_citadel_siege.scene.json** and **claude_grand_contraption.scene.json** for long, multi-stage chain reactions.
- **claude_suspension_bridge.scene.json**, **claude_arch_bridge.scene.json** and **claude_rope_bridge.scene.json** for jointed and destructible bridge structures.
- **claude_rocket_barrage.scene.json**, **claude_bombing_run.scene.json**, **claude_air_raid.scene.json** and **claude_demolition.scene.json** for rockets, aircraft, bombs and large collapses.
- **claude_black_hole.scene.json**, **claude_three_body.scene.json**, **claude_circumbinary.scene.json** and **claude_star_cluster.scene.json** for orbital and gravity experiments.
- **claude_bowling_strike.scene.json**, **claude_card_house.scene.json**, **claude_jenga_chain.scene.json**, **claude_pendulum_wave.scene.json** and **claude_avalanche.scene.json** for smaller mechanical demonstrations.
- Thirty-one **codex_\*** scenes covering airfields, convoys, bridge escapes, marble machines, orbital compositions, ragdoll courses and large vehicle-and-explosion sequences.

Most of the new `claude_*` levels use rich turf and 4 pm daylight, with authored camera data for their intended presentation. Space levels remain flat and use their own space lighting. These scenes can be paused, scrubbed and inspected with the same Replay, Predict and camera tools as existing levels.

## Timed, self-reloading catapults

The new catapult is a real articulated physics machine. Its arm accelerates into a padded stop bar, launches a stone ball, winds back down, and feeds the next ball from its magazine before firing again. Each catapult can have its own firing interval and phase, allowing staggered volleys.

Open **claude_catapult_siege.scene.json** to see four of them in action. Catapults are supplied through the `catapults` asset library for authored levels, but they are not yet listed in the in-application Objects palette.

## GPU water, forecasts and particle effects

Continuous water ripples are now stored in a GPU-binned field supporting up to 32,768 rings. Splash particles are emitted when bodies enter water, while wave and shoreline behavior stays consistent with the Advanced water and Shore wave fade controls introduced in the previous release.

Prediction and forecast paths are now built on the GPU. Long paths remain smooth without spending the same CPU time rebuilding their display geometry each frame.

Every major particle effect now runs through the GPU particle path:

- tornado columns, dust and flying debris;
- rocket flames and complete smoke trails;
- water-entry splashes;
- bomb and demolition blasts.

Rocket trails retain the entire supported 120-second burn, survive replay scrub-back, and start cleanly when a rocket is fired again. Seeking forward no longer draws false smoke across the skipped interval. This longer history uses about 5.3 MiB of video memory plus a matching CPU mirror for all supported trails.

## Curved horizon and world presentation

**Options > Appearance** now includes a curved-horizon amount from 0% to 200%, with 50% as the new default. Terrain, object placement, picking, outlines, debug lines and long or gridded objects all follow the same bend, so interaction stays aligned with what is drawn.

Set the amount to 0% for a flat world. Space scenes and Min-spec graphics also use the flat presentation. Save settings to retain your choice after restarting.

The procedural sky has softer cloud edges without the previous straight, faceted bands. Cascaded shadows are now on by default when ordinary shadows are enabled. Grass reaches the terrain edge more cleanly, and **Reset scene** now clears pressed-grass history instead of carrying old tracks into the reset level.

## Better lower-spec behavior

**Min-spec graphics** now applies one consistent suppression set to the expensive presentation features: grass, water reflection, ambient occlusion, rounded corners, the asteroid belt, contact shadows, depth of field, temporal anti-aliasing, lens flares, cascaded shadows and Advanced water.

While Min-spec graphics is on, the affected switches read Off and are greyed out. Turning Min-spec graphics off restores the choices you previously made rather than overwriting them. **Physics > Simulation > Min-spec physics** remains a separate option affecting the solver rate and iterations.

## Replay and prediction improvements

Replay capture pauses automatically when the entire scene is asleep. Long idle periods therefore stop consuming recording history, and capture resumes on the first active simulation tick.

The repository capture tools can now demonstrate a **Modify velocity** branch as a complete comparison: play the original outcome, rewind, show the velocity edit, and then play the modified outcome with the original retained as blue ghosts. Ordinary application users still create this comparison from **Replay > Predict > Modify velocity**, then choose **Accept Original** or **Accept Modified**.

Water ripples, particle history and forecast paths remain coherent while replaying and scrubbing. A floating ball also loses its residual spin much more quickly after settling in water instead of visibly rotating in place.

## Editor, vehicles and physics fixes

- **Join** and **Unjoin** can weld selected editor objects into one physical piece and separate them again.
- Vehicle wheels can drive across physics bodies, not only terrain.
- Suspension-bridge decks move physically and their cables are drawn.
- Long boxes, large hulls, picking rays and diagnostic lines follow the curved-world presentation.
- Spline-following vehicles bank from actual turning rather than pitching over crests.
- Sleeping ragdolls begin in a settled pose and wake without sinking into their support.
- Large water scenes no longer expose seams between calm and ocean meshes, and authored showcase water levels keep swell below surrounding flat ground.

## Performance and memory notes

The release deliberately trades additional video memory for less per-frame CPU work in the largest scenes. GPU water ripples and forecast paths reserve roughly 151 MiB for their device lifetime, and GPU particles add roughly 11 MiB. The prior release's GPU grass history can still be the largest optional allocation. Use Min-spec graphics or turn off individual features when running near a GPU's memory limit.

Performance varies with scene, resolution and GPU. The showcase integration was built and exercised across Profile, Debug and Automation configurations before merge, including renderer, replay, scene-tour, stress and focused Skarness checks. For this packaged release, the owner manually accepted the application behavior; the release process therefore performed the fresh Release build and package-integrity checks without repeating gameplay automation.

## Known limitations and saved files

- Catapults are available in authored assets and included scenes but not yet in the Objects palette.
- Curved horizon is a visual world presentation, not a change to the underlying physics coordinate system; space scenes remain flat.
- The four-pane view repeats particle compute work for each pane.
- GPU ripples, particle history, forecast paths and grass history increase video-memory use.
- Large recordings can use substantial memory. Save important recordings and Solver Lab sessions before changing scenes or Physics policies.
- Back up personal scenes before saving them with a new version. Editable scenes, recordings, camera paths and Solver Lab sessions are separate files. Personal levels and demo/tutorial copies go in **Documents/SkullbonezCore/Levels**. Older recordings may not be compatible with changed formats; retain the earlier application when you need to open them.

Solver Lab's optional comparison recordings are not included in this download.
